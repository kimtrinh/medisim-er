Privacy

MediSim ER · last updated 5 October 2026

MediSim ER is an educational emergency-medicine simulator. The website is free. The app is free to download, with an optional one-time purchase that unlocks every case. Neither carries advertising, analytics or third-party trackers of any kind. This page describes exactly what it stores and where.

Never enter real patient information. The simulated patients are fictional. Nothing you type into the app should identify a real person.

If you do not sign in

On the website, apart from one anonymous tally when you finish a resuscitation case (described below), nothing you do leaves your browser unless you choose to send it (a suggestion, your email for new cases, or a review — all below). Your cases, scores, settings and training log are kept in your own browser's local storage on your own device. There is no account, and no server receives them. Clearing your browser data deletes them permanently; nobody can recover them for you.

Because that storage belongs to the computer rather than to you, anyone who uses the same browser could see it. That is why the training log stays hidden until you sign in.

If you sign in with Google

Signing in is optional. The seventeen resuscitation cases are free to everyone; signing in opens the rest of the case library, keeps your training log private to you, and lets it follow you between devices. Google tells us only what you approve on its consent screen, which for this app is limited to the three basic scopes openid, email and profile. We never receive your Google password, and we cannot read your mail, contacts, files or anything else in your Google account.

These are held in a database run by Supabase in the United States. Access is enforced by the database itself: each row is readable only by the account that created it. Other users of the simulator cannot see your log, your email, or that you have an account at all.

What is never done

  • Your data is never sold, rented, or shared with advertisers. There are no advertisers.
  • There are no analytics, tracking pixels, fingerprinting, or third-party cookies.
  • No profile is built about you beyond the training log described above.
  • Signing in does not subscribe you to anything. You are emailed only if you tick Email me new cases and updates (it starts unticked), and you can untick it at any time.

Your own API keys

The simulator can optionally run on an AI model of your choosing. If you paste an Anthropic or Google API key into Settings, it is held in your browser for that session only, never written to permanent storage, and never transmitted to us. Your browser sends it directly to the provider you chose. Closing the tab discards it.

The iPhone, iPad and Mac app

The app has no account and no sign-in, no advertising and no tracking. Your cases, scores and settings are kept on your device, and deleting the app deletes them.

  • Microphone and speech. The app listens only while you hold the mic button. What you say is turned into text on your device by Apple's speech recognition; on devices with Apple Intelligence, the on-device model helps read an unclear order. The audio is not sent anywhere.
  • Case logs (off unless you turn them on). With Share case logs on, each finished case sends a record of that case to a private log that only the author reads: the orders given (and, for spoken orders, their text), their timing and how they were graded, plus the app version, device model, system version and language. It carries no account, name, email or device identifier, and it is used only to find and fix mistakes in the simulator.
  • Suggestions. Suggest an improvement sends your note, with which case it was and what happened in it, only when you press Send.
  • Voice recordings (off unless you turn them on). With Save my voice recordings on, recordings of your spoken orders are kept on your device, and leave it only if you share them yourself.
  • The one-time unlock. The purchase that unlocks every case is sold by Apple through the App Store. Apple handles the payment: MediSim ER never sees your name, card or Apple Account. The app learns from Apple only whether the unlock has been bought, so that it can open the cases and restore them on another device. Refunds are handled by Apple.
  • Sharing a case. Can you save this patient? shares a link that names the case by a short code — never your score, your name or anything else about you. If the person you send it to has the app, the case opens in it; if not, a page offering the app opens on kimtrinh.github.io, served by GitHub Pages, which keeps its usual server logs.

The app does not add to the website's anonymous “how many players saved this patient” tally.

How many players saved each patient

When you finish one of the open resuscitation cases, the site adds a single anonymous tally so it can show, for example, “38% of players saved this patient.” The tally holds exactly three things: which case, whether the patient was saved, and the tens digit of the score (so 87 is stored as 8). It carries no account, name, email, device identifier or anything you typed, and it is not linked to you or to your other cases. Nobody — including us — can tell who played. The site only ever reads back the totals for each case, and shows a percentage only once a case has been played at least 30 times. This is a count of case outcomes, not analytics: nothing else about your visit is recorded.

The share card after a case (“Can you save this patient?”) is made on your own device. It shows the case and your result, never your name, and it goes nowhere unless you share it yourself.

Optional research logs

Settings contains a switch, off by default, for sharing de-identified case logs — the orders given and how they were graded — so that mistakes in the simulator can be found and fixed. Those records carry no account, name or email, and email addresses, phone numbers and identifier-shaped text are stripped from them before they are sent. Leaving the switch off shares nothing. Sharing requires being signed in, so that only real people can write; the record itself still carries no account.

Suggestions you send

Suggest an improvement, in the debrief and beside any order, sends a note only when you press Send, whether or not the switch above is on — pressing Send is the consent. A note holds what you typed, which case it was, and, if you opened it from an order, that order and how the simulator answered it. Like the case logs it carries no account, name or email, and the same identifier-shaped text is stripped before it leaves. If this copy of the site has nowhere to send it, the form says so and offers the note as a file for you to download instead; nothing is kept to send later.

News by email, for people who sign in

When you sign in, the sign-in box offers Email me new cases and updates, unticked. If you tick it, we record that choice and its date next to your account, and may email the address your Google account gave us about new cases and changes to the simulator — nothing else, and no one else's news. Untick it in Settings, or use the unsubscribe link in any email, and the emails stop. If you never tick it, your address is used only for your account.

The new-case email list

The debrief offers Email me new cases. If you fill it in, we keep your email address, the role you picked, which case you signed up from, and the date. We use them for one thing: to email you when new cases are added. Your address is not sold, rented or shared, and it is never added to anything else. Every email we send will carry a way to unsubscribe, and you can also ask to be removed at the address below; you are taken off the list and the address deleted. The list is stored in Supabase and can be read only by the simulator's author.

Reviews

At the end of a case the debrief asks Did this case help you? If you send a review, we keep the name you gave, the role and star rating if you chose them, your words, which case it was, the date, and whether you ticked You may show my name, role, rating and review on the MediSim ER site. Your name, role, rating and review appear on the site only if you ticked that box; otherwise they are read by the simulator's author and no one else. Nothing else is attached to a review: no email, no account. Reviews are stored in Supabase and can be read only by the simulator's author. To have a review taken down or deleted, write to the address below with the name you used and roughly when you sent it.

Other services the page contacts

  • Supabase — sign-in and your training log, only when you are signed in; the new-case email list, only when you sign up for it; and reviews, only when you send one.
  • Cloudflare — the collector that receives a suggestion when you press Send, and the case logs when the switch above is on. It stores nothing itself; it files the record in a private repository and keeps no record of who sent it.
  • Anthropic or Google — only if you supply your own API key, and only from your browser.
  • Wikimedia Commons — some of the study images are hosted there.
  • jsDelivr and cdnjs — code libraries, loaded only when needed.
  • GitHub Pages — serves the site itself.

The page's content security policy blocks connections to anywhere else.

Children

MediSim ER is intended for clinicians and clinical students. It is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 16.

Keeping, and deleting

Your training log is kept until you ask for it to be removed. To delete your account and everything attached to it, email the address below from the account you signed up with; it is removed within 30 days. You can also sign out at any time, which leaves nothing about you on that device.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top changes with it. Continuing to use the simulator after that means the new version applies.
